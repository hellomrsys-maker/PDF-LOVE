Make Image Smaller

Photo file sizes have grown faster than the limits that constrain them: a modern phone shot is routinely 6 MB, and the form you are uploading it to wants under 1 MB. Quality is the dial, and you should be able to see what it costs.

Your file never leaves this device. Pick a file and watch the Network tab — nothing fires while it is being processed. No upload, no account, no daily limit.

How to use Make Image Smaller

  1. Choose the image.
  2. Move the quality slider and watch the estimated size change.
  3. Compare the preview against the original.
  4. Download when the size is under your limit and it still looks right.

What to know

Compression discards detail the eye is least likely to miss, so the honest way to choose is by looking rather than by trusting a percentage. Photographs tolerate aggressive settings well; screenshots, line art and anything with text or sharp edges show artefacts far sooner — for those, resizing the dimensions or converting to PNG is usually the better answer. Around 80 per cent quality is where most photographs stop looking any different.

Common questions

What quality setting should I pick?

Around 80% is invisible on most photographs. Judge by the preview, not the number.

My screenshot looks blotchy.

Sharp edges and text compress badly. Use PNG, or reduce the dimensions instead.

Is the original changed?

No. You download a new file; the original on your device is untouched.
